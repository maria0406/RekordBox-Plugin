# Build the practice crate from Creative Commons labels

Set Prep Copilot can legally get house audio at volume from two places. The first is the **Internet Archive netlabels collection**: 77k releases, about 2.9k tagged house and 6.2k tagged techno, with FLAC files and no API key needed. The second is **Jamendo**, which has about 2.2k house, 0.4k deep house and 2.2k techno tracks, all full-length. In both, only the **CC BY / CC BY-SA subset** is clean for a product that might ever make money and lets users export mixes. In Jamendo's case that subset is about 1,100 house, deep house and techno tracks. Everything built for video creators fails this use case on license grounds, even with big catalogs and free tiers: Pixabay, NCS, Epidemic, Artlist, Uppbeat, the SoundCloud API and Beatport streaming. Their terms forbid standalone redistribution, machine analysis or storing files, or they gate access behind partner deals. For the MVP, ingest a few hundred hand-vetted CC BY/BY-SA house tracks from IA netlabels, and Jamendo if needed, into your own store, and run your own analyzer on them. To scale, add a small owned or label-licensed "starter crate", the way Crossfader and Algoriddim's djay do. The existing Spotify integration can stay, but only as a metadata and library bridge. Spotify's developer policy now forbids analyzing Spotify audio or mixing it with other audio, and the audio-features endpoints are gone for new apps. Spotify cannot feed the analysis pipeline. One question decides which tracks are usable: will the app ever have a paid tier or ads? NonCommercial (NC) tracks are about 65% of netlabel releases and most of Jamendo's house. They are usable only if the answer is no.

## Two open catalogs hold almost all usable full-length house

The **Internet Archive netlabels collection** is the deepest pool. A live query returned **77,019 audio items**. Subject-tag counts were **2,872 house, 6,216 techno, 3,862 minimal and 745 "deep house" or "tech house"** ([IA advancedsearch](https://archive.org/advancedsearch.php)). Each item is a whole release (an EP or album), so the number of tracks is several times higher. The tags are free text, though, so these counts are approximate. The collection holds the "complete, freely downloadable and streamable catalogs" of virtual record labels, including deep minimal, acid house and techno imprints ([IA netlabels RSS](https://archive.org/services/collection-rss.php?mediatype=audio&collection=netlabels)). A sample release's metadata lists **FLAC, WAV, VBR MP3 and Ogg files, plus precomputed Essentia analysis-descriptor files** ([archive.org/metadata/yarn014](https://archive.org/metadata/yarn014)). You can use those descriptors to cross-check your own BPM and key output. The drawback is licensing: **49,881 of the 77,019 items (about 65%) carry a by-nc license URL** ([IA advancedsearch](https://archive.org/advancedsearch.php)).

**Jamendo** is the other large catalog of full-length tracks. Openverse indexes **644,709 Jamendo items** across all genres ([Openverse sources](https://openverse.org/sources)). We counted tags in the MTG-Jamendo research snapshot of the catalog. It has **2,169 house, 427 deephouse and 2,179 techno tracks**, with mean durations of 4.5 to 5.2 minutes ([MTG-Jamendo genre stats](https://github.com/MTG/mtg-jamendo-dataset/blob/master/stats/raw_30s_cleantags_50artists/genre.tsv)). That length is enough for real intros and outros. Joining those tags with the per-track license file gives **4,215 house, deep house or techno tracks, of which 824 are BY-SA and 318 are BY: 1,142 tracks that allow both commercial use and derivatives** ([audio_licenses.txt](https://github.com/MTG/mtg-jamendo-dataset/blob/master/audio_licenses.txt)). The **Free Music Archive** adds a smaller pool. Its research dump lists 1,482 house and 2,140 techno tracks, but only **435 of the 3,366 house and techno tracks are commercial-OK**, and none are CC0 ([fma_metadata.zip](https://os.unil.cloud.switch.ch/fma/fma_metadata.zip)). FMA also shut down its API ([FMA app developers](https://freemusicarchive.org/app-developers)). Use it only for manual curation.

Other sources fail on volume or shape. Freesound has about 591k items, but they are loops and one-shots. A "tech house" query returned a 7.6-second clip ([Openverse API](https://api.openverse.org/v1/audio/)), which is no good for practicing phrasing. **Audius** has the most modern catalog of DJ-style house, and its API returns **BPM and musical key fields** (for example, 122 BPM, G minor, "Deep House") ([Audius API](https://api.audius.co/v1)). However, the house tracks we sampled were **"All rights reserved" and not downloadable**. Since July 2025, third-party use is governed by a custom Open Music License whose terms we did not read ([Audius blog](https://blog.audius.co/posts/audius-terms-of-service-update)). Treat Audius as a source to watch, not a catalog you can ship.

## Platform terms, not CC licenses, are the real constraint

Every Creative Commons license allows you to reproduce and share tracks unmodified, as long as you give attribution ([CC BY-ND 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nd/4.0/legalcode.en)). Computing BPM, key, beat grids and phrase boundaries produces **factual measurements, not "Adapted Material"**, so analysis is effectively free of risk under any CC license, including ND. US fair-use precedent supports this kind of transformative, non-substitutive copying ([Authors Guild v. HathiTrust](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/authorsguild-hathitrust-2dcir2014.pdf)). So does the EU text-and-data-mining exception (DSM Art. 4), subject to machine-readable opt-outs ([Kluwer Copyright Blog](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/the-new-copyright-directive-text-and-data-mining-articles-3-and-4/)). The license splits that matter are these two:

**ND:** since version 4.0, users can make a mix of ND tracks privately but cannot share it ([CC certificate course](https://creativecommons.org/course/cc-cert-edu/unit-4-using-cc-licenses-and-cc-licensed-works/4-4-remixing-cc-licensed-work/)). A beatmatched, time-stretched, EQ'd recording is very likely an adaptation. No CC source or court has ruled on audio-only DJ mixes, so this is an inference.

**NC:** the test is whether a use is "primarily intended for or directed towards commercial advantage" ([CC NonCommercial interpretation](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/NonCommercial_interpretation)). A freemium app whose paid tier draws its value from the music sits in a grey zone, and probably on the wrong side of it.

Platform terms stack further restrictions on top. **Jamendo's API is free only for non-commercial use**, and it defines commercial to include "any revenue arising from affiliation programs or advertising". It also says apps "must not be specifically designed to cache the content nor offering an offline access" ([Jamendo API ToU](https://devportal.jamendo.com/api_terms_of_use)). That clashes with a practice app that wants files on local disk for analysis and low-latency mixing. Jamendo also requires per-track backlinks and credit to Jamendo as the provider. One question stays unresolved: does fetching CC BY tracks from Jamendo's public site, rather than the API, escape the API overlay? Settle it with counsel or a Jamendo Licensing quote before shipping commercially. The **SoundCloud API** forbids file-save features and offline access. It also bans using content as "input to ... machine intelligence technologies", which arguably reaches ML beat trackers ([SoundCloud API ToU](https://developers.soundcloud.com/docs/api/terms-of-use)). It now serves only HLS AAC at 96 or 160 kbps ([SoundCloud dev blog](https://developers.soundcloud.com/blog/api-streaming-urls/)).

Production-music libraries fail even more clearly. Epidemic's license bars use "where the Licensed Work(s) constitute a primary value", any "music recognition systems or any machine learning application", and apps "enabling end users to synchronize or otherwise combine" tracks ([Epidemic Single-Track License V8](https://www.epidemicsound.com/staticfiles/legacy/20/documents/SingleTrackLicensesV8.pdf)). That is a near-exact description of Set Prep Copilot. Artlist bans "separate files for listening, viewing, downloading" and machine-learning datasets ([Artlist license](https://artlist.io/help-center/privacy-terms/artlist-license/)). Pixabay is not CC0. Its license forbids distributing content "on a Standalone basis" ([Pixabay license summary](https://pixabay.com/service/license-summary/)), and its API has **no music endpoint** ([Pixabay API docs](https://pixabay.com/api/docs/)). NCS requires a paid agreement for commercial use ([NCS usage policy](https://ncs.io/usage-policy)). Beatport streaming is reserved for approved licensees. Its terms forbid reproducing or distributing API content or using it commercially without express permission ([Beatport T&C](https://support.beatport.com/hc/en-us/articles/4414997837716-Terms-and-Conditions)). Partners such as DJ.Studio may analyze streamed tracks, but users "cannot publish mixes containing streaming files" ([DJ.Studio help](https://help.dj.studio/en/articles/12332505-beatport-beatsource-streaming-vs-shop-in-dj-studio)).

The practical risks lie elsewhere than the license text. CC licenses are irrevocable but **carry no warranty that the uploader held the rights** ([CC FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/)). An operator-curated catalog does not get the DMCA user-upload safe harbor, so laundered uploads become your liability. Jamendo also registers some CC BY-NC-ND tracks in YouTube Content ID ([Jamendo support](https://support-artist.jamendo.com/youtube-content-id)). Users who post practice mixes can therefore get claims even when every license is valid. Mixcloud, which holds blanket PRO licenses for DJ sets, is the safer place to send them ([Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/mixcloud-pacts-with-eu-licensor-ice-for-12m-dj-sets/)).

## An MVP needs a few hundred vetted tracks, not the whole archive

A beginner tool does not need 10,000 tracks. It needs a few hundred clean, full-length, 4/4 tracks at 118–130 BPM with clear 16- and 32-bar intros. The MVP pipeline follows from the constraints above. Query IA netlabels for house, techno and minimal subjects with `licenseurl` set to CC BY, BY-SA or CC0, and prefer items with FLAC. Optionally add the 1,142 BY/BY-SA Jamendo house and techno tracks, using the MTG-Jamendo metadata as a pre-filter. Fetch individual files rather than the 508 GB dataset, which is itself licensed "solely for non-commercial research" ([MTG-Jamendo GitHub](https://github.com/MTG/mtg-jamendo-dataset)). Then run the app's own analyzer (`audio_analysis.py`) and keep only tracks that pass a tempo and beat-strength check. Source tags are artist-supplied and noisy: an Openverse "house" query also matched a film-score track ([Openverse API](https://api.openverse.org/v1/audio/)).

Store a license record with every track: title, artist, source URL, license name, version and URL, retrieval date, and a snapshot of the license page. CC attribution can be satisfied "in any reasonable manner based on the medium" ([CC BY-ND 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nd/4.0/legalcode.en)). Show Title, Author, Source and License (TASL) credits on the player, keep a credits page, and auto-generate a tracklist that flags modifications for any exported mix ([CC attribution practices](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/Recommended_practices_for_attribution)). Add two flags per track, `commercial_ok` and `can_share_adaptation`. Disable mix export whenever an ND track is loaded. Remember that BY-SA forces a shared mix to carry BY-SA, which makes it incompatible with NC tracks in the same mix. Run a fingerprint check against commercial catalogs, and build a delisting kill-switch for laundering complaints.

If the team commits to a **permanently free, ad-free** product, the NC tracks open up. That is roughly two-thirds of netlabel releases and most of Jamendo's house, and the Jamendo API's free 35k-requests-per-month tier becomes usable ([Jamendo licensing help](https://help-licensing.jamendo.com/hc/en-us/articles/20699346005661-Jamendo-API)). That decision is cheap to make now and expensive to reverse after users have built habits around NC tracks.

For QA, keep a separate, never-shipped benchmark set. Use GiantSteps Tempo v2 (664 Beatport previews) for BPM ([GiantSteps Tempo](https://github.com/GiantSteps/giantsteps-tempo-dataset)). Use the Beatport EDM Key set (1,486 excerpts) for key ([Zenodo 1101082](https://zenodo.org/records/1101082)). Use Raveform's CC BY 4.0 beat, downbeat and EDM-section annotations on 1,423 tracks for phrase and drop detection; you have to source that audio yourself ([TISMIR](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/10.5334/tismir.288)). No dataset combines full-length CC house audio with phrase annotations. Hand-annotating 50–100 of your own CC BY catalog tracks would fill that gap and produce a redistributable asset.

## Scaling means owning or licensing a crate, as the DJ vendors do

The industry pattern points the same way. Serato DJ Lite ships **six demo tracks, three of them house** ([BPM Music blog](https://blog.bpmmusic.io/news/serato-dj-lite-1-3-2-introduces-beatgrids-demo-tracks-and-more/)). Crossfader's free Rekordbox course runs on its own "**100+ royalty-free DJ-ready tracks**" pack plus Tidal and Beatport trials ([Crossfader](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/free/)). Algoriddim offers "djay Music", a free in-app catalog sourced "from global artists and record labels" ([Algoriddim help](https://help.algoriddim.com/user-manual/djay-ios/music-library/djay-music)). None of these publish reusable terms. They are direct deals.

Set Prep Copilot can copy this cheaply in two ways. **Commissioning** exclusive house tracks costs about **€199–€499 each, with all rights transferred** ([House of Tracks](https://houseoftracks.com/faq/how-much-does-a-ghost-production-cost)), so a pedagogically designed 20–30 track crate costs roughly €5k–€15k. That crate can have clean 8/16/32-bar phrasing and a deliberate spread of keys and BPMs. It is the only option that cleanly covers offline caching, tutorials, shared mixes and any future ML work. **Label or artist deals** cost less up front. The Traktor DJ 2 demo artists (Cavern Floor, Deep Matter, Indigo Dust) have already licensed tracks for this exact use ([Internet Archive mirror](https://archive.org/details/ni-traktor-dj-2-demo-tracks)), and so have the CC netlabels already in your catalog. Any contract should explicitly grant in-app streaming and download, offline caching, storage of derived analysis, and tutorial use.

**Jamendo Licensing** (pricing by quote) is the route for unlocking NC tracks commercially ([Jamendo API ToU](https://devportal.jamendo.com/api_terms_of_use)). Epidemic's Enterprise tier is the only production-library path, and it would need a custom carve-out from its ML and standalone clauses ([Epidemic developers](https://www.epidemicsound.com/business/developers/)). AI generation is a weaker supplement. Suno's paid tier reportedly grants a commercial license rather than ownership, and Udio output cannot leave its platform. Both claims come from secondary sources ([Dubspot](https://blog.dubspot.com/ai-music-licensing-explained-2026)).

## Spotify stays as a metadata bridge, never an audio source

The Spotify Developer Policy (effective May 15, 2025) says to "not analyze the Spotify Content ... for any purpose". It also bans ingesting Spotify content into ML models, and bans any system that would "segue, mix, re-mix, or overlap any Spotify Content with any other audio content" ([Spotify Developer Policy](https://developer.spotify.com/policy)). Separately, Spotify cut new apps' access to audio-features and audio-analysis on **November 27, 2024** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/27/spotify-cuts-developer-access-to-several-of-its-recommendation-features/)). There is still no replacement in 2026 ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/birrings/spotifys-audiofeatures-api-died-in-2024-heres-what-i-built-to-replace-it-3dn3)).

For the current codebase, this means `spotify_client.py` should be limited to auth, search, playlists and track identity. Never route preview clips or streams into `audio_analysis.py`. The Spotify-only results view should show BPM and key from GetSongBPM (via `getsongbpm_client.py`, under that service's own terms, which we did not review) rather than implying the app analyzed Spotify audio. Spotify's real value is as a bridge. A learner practices on the CC crate, then sees their own Spotify playlists annotated with third-party BPM and key so they can plan a real set. Actual beat-grid and phrase work happens on files the user owns, analyzed locally, just as the Rekordbox integration already does. Local analysis of lawfully owned files is the lowest-risk design. Uploading those files to a server adds copy-retention exposure, which the *Bartz v. Anthropic* ruling treated as decisive ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/06/two-us-courts-address-fair-use-in-genai-training-cases)).

## Conclusion

The question changes once the legal layers are separated. Audio analysis itself is barely regulated: CC licenses, fair use and EU TDM rules all tolerate extracting BPM, key and beat grids. The binding constraints are the distribution model (caching, export and monetization) and the private terms that platforms layer over open licenses. A source's catalog size matters less than whether it adds such an overlay. That is why the plainest option, label-curated CC BY files from the Internet Archive, beats every polished API. Jamendo becomes a stronger second source than its API suggests if the team settles whether its terms reach files fetched outside the API.

The team should make two decisions now, because they set the size of the legal catalog. The first is whether the app will ever monetize, which sets whether the usable pool is about 35% or 100% of open tracks. The second is whether users can export mixes, which sets whether ND tracks are allowed. The most durable long-term asset is not a bigger open catalog. It is a small owned crate built for teaching, paired with phrase annotations that nobody else has published.
